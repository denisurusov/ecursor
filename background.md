# Introduction

Enterprises are adopting Generative AI (GenAI) to transform their processes.

Engineering processes - centered around Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC) - are the best/easiest to start with, given the high degree of existing automation, tooling adoption and captured and codified industry knowledge.

In the Financial Services industries there are other workflows - AML reviews, trade flows, client operation flows - that are a) interlinked, more complex and much less automated. We'll keep this in mind, while using SDLC flows as the working surface.

## Background

Today, a number of Gen AI platforms (harness.io, 8090.ai, cursor.ai) are makign signigicant inroads into creating true agentic SDLC platforms, aiming to cover the entire value chain, converting requirements into operational products faster and more precisely.

These advancements offer a significant opportunity to improve the entire value chain, especially by making the SDLC more cost-efficient and boosting engineering productivity.

However, a successful GenAI strategy involves more than just adopting tools. It requires a fundamental shift in organizational culture, processes, workforce skills and underlying technology infrastructure capabilities.

## GenAI Adoption Tiers

The adoption of Generative AI (GenAI) in engineering can be categorized into distinct tiers, each reflecting a unique level of maturity and organizational impact. Understanding these tiers is critical for aligning BNBs strategic objectives with its GenAI ambitions.

- Tier 1 (Full Automation): GenAI automates the entire SDLC, enabling business users to specify feature requirements and receive fully deployed, tested products. Engineering roles shift to a supportive, background function, with GenAI driving most development tasks
- Tier 2 (Enhanced Collaboration): Small, highly skilled engineering teams leverage GenAI to collaborate directly with business stakeholders, delivering tailored products. This approach accelerates delivery, reduces team size, and enhances efficiency, potentially disrupting traditional SaaS and software vendor markets
- Lower Tiers (Partial Integration): Various organizations exhibit a range of maturity levels, with use cases reflecting partial GenAI integration and evolving development practices.
